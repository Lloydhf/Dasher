# Ascent 0.5 design

## Core loop

Vote for a tower, watch or skip its short introduction, then climb eight sectors using jumps and one Updraft. Falling onto an earlier piece of terrain allows a physical recovery; there are no saved checkpoints. Contact with a dangerous obstacle or falling into the void returns the player to the base. The round clock continues.

## Difficulty

Three authored towers use small footholds, thin balance beams, wall ledges, moving shuttles, temporary footholds and rotating environmental obstacles. Each has 64 main-route platforms, eight required Updraft transitions and fourteen branching choices. Most normal gaps are five to eight studs edge-to-edge. Section colors communicate route structure. Updraft rises have clear space above their launch area. Its 3.25-second cooldown and one use per airborne sequence make it a route/recovery resource rather than unlimited flight.

## Independent competition

No combat, sabotage or body blocking. Up to ten racers have separate starting slots. Spectating, map votes, finish order and weekly scores remain. Valid alternate routes can finish without touching an exact sequence of rest pads. The first finisher caps remaining time at 90 seconds; a race also ends when all active entrants have finished or withdrawn. Paid advantages are not offered. Player avatars stay unchanged except for optional cosmetic effects.

## Progression

Eight once-per-round sector claims provide up to 24 coins and 200 XP. A finish adds 14 coins and 100 XP, with rank bonuses 6/3/1 coins and winner 50 XP. Daily and weekly rewards remain. Levels use 500 XP intervals. The new courses use CourseVersion 5, preserving balances, owned cosmetics and XP while archiving old records.

## Presentation

Nunito / Fredoka One typography restores the earlier game's style. The garden atrium contains a tiered fountain, hanging greenery, covered stalls, seating and four NPC services. Restrained lobby text, one ability control, compact clock/stage/level HUD, a 6-second lobby introduction and 4-second map view, and skip/reduced-motion controls keep the route visible. Movement particles have distinct textures, lifetimes and trajectories; they stop emitting when idle and cull at distance. Animated shop previews show each cosmetic's identity before purchase.

## Playtest questions

Measure first-sector success, failures by obstacle, time to first cosmetic and voluntary replays. Increasing size alone does not make an engaging route. No player retention or commercial result is claimed from automated tests. Human difficulty and mobile touch feedback remain necessary.
